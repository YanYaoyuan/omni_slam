\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The results support a bounded use of visibility evidence: improving the geometry of an existing map while preserving its identities. The operation is interpretable because every removal follows recorded mask votes and measured-depth agreement. It also has clear failure modes. A missing object cannot be recovered by deletion. A merged object can remain contaminated when its assigned masks consistently support the extra surface. Unknown regions and occluded points intentionally receive no negative vote.

The comparison with SOR indicates that semantic evidence and geometric sparsity are different signals. \ours{} improves three scenes more than SOR and preserves existing matches relative to the baseline, but does not obtain the best primary macro mean. Our ablations test the depth gate and refutation minimum; they do not isolate \ours{} against the point-ratio filter of another complete mapper under identical object membership. Such a comparison remains necessary for a broader mechanism-level superiority claim.

The four new SceneNN sequences provide within-dataset evidence. The evaluation uses given poses and a class-agnostic observed-domain metric; it does not establish cross-dataset generalization, semantic-query accuracy, or robustness to pose error. Sparse robot LiDAR and dense RGB-D have different visibility coverage. Consequently, the archived robot demonstrations and the controlled geometry benchmark answer different questions.

\paragraph{Revisable ownership as future work.} Fixed ownership limits the present module: an erroneous historical association can make both accumulated geometry and its assigned masks support the same mistake. A next research hypothesis is to retain observation provenance, propose a local reversal of an erroneous merge, and validate it with later views not used to construct the proposal. The acceptance test would recompute correspondence rather than inherit the disputed ownership. Such a module would change instance structure and require separate controls for ordinary geometric splitting and multi-view consensus. It has not been implemented or evaluated here; no current score or robot figure measures this proposed capability.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented visibility-guided geometry revision for object-level mapping. Depth-consistent mask evidence supports, refutes, or abstains on source-point ownership while the existing identities and semantics remain fixed. Controlled evaluation improves the strong baseline in all four new SceneNN sequences and preserves all 66 existing matches. Object-level analysis explains both geometric gains and a threshold-sensitive deficit relative to SOR. These findings establish a concrete role for revisiting accumulated geometry and identify the evidence limitations that further refinement must address.
