\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Object-Centric Mapping with Foundation Models}
ConceptGraphs~\cite{gu2024conceptgraphs} associates observations from posed images into an object-centric map and scene graph. We reuse its association core in a shared-input comparison and in our strong baseline. SAM~2~\cite{ravi2024sam2} and SigLIP~\cite{zhai2023siglip} supply the masks and descriptors used by all compared methods. OVO~\cite{martins2025ovo} combines online segment tracking with descriptor merging for SLAM; its released infrastructure contributes to our shared frontend. These components establish the input object representation rather than the geometric intervention evaluated here.

\subsection{Consensus, Evidence, and Geometric Filtering}
MaskClustering~\cite{yan2024maskclustering} uses view consensus to construct a mask graph for open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation. Its released post-processing already includes point detection-ratio filtering~\cite{maskclustering_pinned_code}. Multi-view accumulation and point filtering are therefore established mechanisms. OVI-MAP~\cite{deng2026ovimap} separates instance reconstruction from semantic inference, while VOIM~\cite{song2026voim} accumulates voxel evidence before deciding labels and instances.

Our focus differs in the controlled intervention: source-point geometry is revised after object identities and observation memberships have been fixed. The proposed evidence domain requires an observation of the same object, direct depth agreement, and a decisive mask relation. A point with no decisive observations is retained. The support/refutation ratio is not, by itself, a new principle of evidence fusion. The experimental question is whether this particular evidence domain improves the geometry of an unchanged mapper. We compare against SOR and adapted shared-input mapping cores; we do not infer a ranking against their original complete pipelines.

\paragraph{Refinable maps and object correspondence.} Fu et al.~\cite{fu2026refinable} update point confidence using masks, depth, and visibility, and prune unreliable or outdated geometry. This is a direct conceptual neighbor: visibility-conditioned point revision itself is not a novelty claim of this paper. OASIS-Map~\cite{oh2026oasis} uses dense semantic correspondences for object association across mapping sessions. Our evaluated module neither re-identifies moved objects nor revises instance membership. Its narrower subject is fixed-ownership geometric filtering, whose advantage over equally controlled point-ratio filters remains untested.
